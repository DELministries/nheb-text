Lev 13:1 The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,
Lev 13:2 “When someone has a swelling or rash or bright spot on his skin that may be an infectious skin disease, he must be brought to Aaron the priest or to one of his sons who is a priest.
Lev 13:3 The priest must then examine the infection on his skin, and if the hair in the infection has turned white and the sore appears to be deeper than the skin, it is a skin disease. After the priest examines him, he must pronounce him unclean.
Lev 13:4 If it is a white spot on the skin and does not appear to be deeper than the skin, and the hair in it has not turned white, the priest shall isolate the infected person for seven days.
Lev 13:5 The priest must then reexamine it on the seventh day, and if he sees that the infection is unchanged and has not spread on the skin, the priest must isolate him for another seven days.
Lev 13:6 The priest will examine it again on the seventh day, and if the sore has faded and has not spread on the skin, the priest shall pronounce him clean; it is a rash. The person must wash his clothes and be clean.
Lev 13:7 But if the rash spreads further on his skin after he has shown himself to the priest for his cleansing, he must present himself again to the priest.
Lev 13:8 The priest must then reexamine it, and if the rash has spread on the skin, the priest must pronounce him unclean; it is a skin disease.
Lev 13:9 When anyone has a skin disease, he must be brought to the priest.
Lev 13:10 The priest will then examine it, and if there is a white swelling on the skin that has turned the hair white, and there is raw flesh in the swelling,
Lev 13:11 it is a chronic skin disease, so the priest must pronounce him unclean. He need not isolate him, for he is unclean.
Lev 13:12 If, however, the skin disease breaks out all over his skin so that it covers all the skin of the infected person from head to foot, as far as the priest can see,
Lev 13:13 the priest must then examine it, and if the disease has covered his entire body, he is to pronounce the infected person clean. Since it has all turned white, he is clean.
Lev 13:14 But whenever raw flesh appears in it, he will be unclean.
Lev 13:15 When the priest sees the raw flesh, he must pronounce him unclean—it is diseased.
Lev 13:16 But if the raw flesh changes and turns white, he must go to the priest.
Lev 13:17 The priest will then reexamine it, and if the infection has turned white, the priest is to pronounce the infected person clean; then he is clean.
Lev 13:18 When a boil appears on someone’s skin and it heals,
Lev 13:19 and a white swelling or a reddish-white spot develops where the boil was, he must show himself to the priest.
Lev 13:20 The priest will then examine it, and if it appears to be beneath the skin and the hair in it has turned white, the priest shall pronounce him unclean; it is a diseased infection that has broken out in the boil.
Lev 13:21 But if the priest examines it, and there is no white hair in it, it is not beneath the skin and has faded, then the priest shall isolate him for seven days.
Lev 13:22 If it is spreading further on the skin, the priest must pronounce him unclean; it is an infection.
Lev 13:23 But if the spot remains unchanged and has not spread, it is only the scar from the boil, and the priest shall pronounce him clean.
Lev 13:24 When a body has a burn on its skin and the raw area of the burn becomes reddish-white or white,
Lev 13:25 the priest must examine it, and if the hair in the spot has turned white and the spot appears to be deeper than the skin, it is a disease that has broken out in the burn. The priest must pronounce him unclean; it is a diseased infection.
Lev 13:26 But if the priest examines it and there is no white hair in the spot, and it is not beneath the skin but has faded, the priest shall isolate him for seven days.
Lev 13:27 The priest must then reexamine it on the seventh day, and if it has spread further on the skin, the priest must pronounce him unclean; it is a diseased infection.
Lev 13:28 But if the spot is unchanged and has not spread on the skin, and it has faded, it is a swelling from the burn, and the priest is to pronounce him clean; for it is only the scar from the burn.
Lev 13:29 When a man or woman has an infection on the head or chin,
Lev 13:30 the priest is to examine the infection, and if it appears to be deeper than the skin and the hair in it is yellow and thin, the priest must pronounce him unclean; it is a scaly outbreak, an infectious disease of the head or chin.
Lev 13:31 But if the priest examines the scaly infection and it does not appear to be deeper than the skin, and there is no black hair in it, then the priest shall isolate the infected person for seven days.
Lev 13:32 The priest is to reexamine the infection on the seventh day, and if the scaly outbreak has not spread and there is no yellow hair in it, and it does not appear to be deeper than the skin,
Lev 13:33 then the individual must shave himself except for the scaly area. Then the priest shall isolate him for another seven days.
Lev 13:34 The priest must examine the scaly outbreak on the seventh day, and if it has not spread on the skin and does not appear to be deeper than the skin, the priest is to pronounce him clean. He must wash his clothes, and he will be clean.
Lev 13:35 If, however, the scaly outbreak spreads further on the skin after his cleansing,
Lev 13:36 then the priest is to examine him, and if the scaly outbreak has spread on the skin, the priest need not look for yellow hair; the person is unclean.
Lev 13:37 If, however, as far as he can see, the scaly outbreak is unchanged and black hair has grown in it, then it has healed. He is clean, and the priest is to pronounce him clean.
Lev 13:38 When a man or a woman has white spots on the skin,
Lev 13:39 the priest is to examine them, and if the spots are dull white, it is a harmless rash that has broken out on the skin; the person is clean.
Lev 13:40 Now if a man’s head is bare, so that he is balding in back, he is still clean.
Lev 13:41 If his hairline recedes and he is bald on his forehead, he is still clean.
Lev 13:42 But if there is a reddish-white infection on the bald head or forehead, it is an infectious disease breaking out on it.
Lev 13:43 The priest is to examine it, and if the swelling of the infection on his bald head or forehead is reddish-white like a skin disease,
Lev 13:44 the man is diseased. He is unclean. The priest must pronounce him unclean because of the infection on his head.
Lev 13:45 A diseased person must wear torn clothes and let his hair hang loose, he must cover his mouth and cry out, ‘Unclean, unclean!’
Lev 13:46 As long as he has the infection, he remains unclean. He must live in isolation in a place outside the camp.
Lev 13:47 If any fabric is contaminated with mildew, whether a wool or linen garment,
Lev 13:48 any weave or knit of linen or wool, or anything made of leather,
Lev 13:49 and if the mark in the fabric, leather, weave, knit, or leather article is green or red, it is contaminated with mildew and must be shown to the priest.
Lev 13:50 And the priest is to examine the mildew and quarantine the contaminated fabric for seven days.
Lev 13:51 He must then reexamine it on the seventh day, and if the mildew has spread in the fabric, weave, knit, or leather, then regardless of how it is used, it is a harmful mildew; the article is unclean.
Lev 13:52 He must burn the fabric, weave, or knit, whether the contaminated item is wool or linen or leather. Since the mildew is harmful, the article must be burned up.
Lev 13:53 But when the priest reexamines it, if the mildew has not spread in the fabric, weave, knit, or in any article of leather,
Lev 13:54 the priest is to command the contaminated article to be washed and isolated for another seven days.
Lev 13:55 After it has been washed, the priest must reexamine it, and if the mildewed article has not changed in appearance, it is unclean. Even though the mildew has not spread, you must burn it, whether the rot is on the front or back.
Lev 13:56 But if the priest examines it and the mildew has faded after it has been washed, he must cut the contaminated section out of the fabric, leather, weave, or knit.
Lev 13:57 Then if it reappears in the fabric, weave, or knit, or on any leather article, it is spreading. You must burn the contaminated article.
Lev 13:58 If the mildew disappears from the fabric, weave, or knit, or any article of leather after washing, then it is to be washed again, and it will be clean.
Lev 13:59 This is the law concerning a mildew contamination in wool or linen fabric, weave, or knit, or any article of leather, for pronouncing it clean or unclean.”
